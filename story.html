How it began

The Story of “The Story of the Magic Pick”

“The Story of the Magic Pick” was a single I put out in 1973. It is the precursor of Magic Pick, the Musical. After it was released, I spent the next couple of years working on a “spin-off” musical which I called The Curse of the Magic Pick and it had a very short run (one performance) in Lennoxville, Quebec by an amateur theatre group called Earthstage. The single never got any action (it had the misfortune of being released the same week as another “novelty” single also distributed by Polydor) and I never did anything else with the musical and turned my attention to being a bass-player-for-hire, touring the country and having no fixed address. Then recently when I told this story to a friend who writes and directs musicals, she encouraged me to dig it up, and it really was an excavation, finding scraps of paper with lyrics and cassette tapes of the songs in progress. No script - the only copy went to the director and I never got it back.

So this is what I did last summer, re-learning 23 songs and assembling them in a music software program called Ableton. After living with these songs for the last few months, I’m encouraged that 50 years later, there’s still a worthy message. Actually very timely.

In the winter of 1973, I was in Andre Perry’s Montreal studio (before he built Le Studio in Morin Heights) where we were recording a song called “Don’t Forget Your Mother” with members of Frank Zappa’s Mothers, The Blues Brothers and Manhattan Transfer. A boys’ choir from a local private school had been engaged to sing on the chorus and when there were some technical issues in the control room, I was asked to go out into the studio and calm down the kids who were getting restless. I borrowed a guitar from a Quebec artist (Tex Lecor) who was recording in the adjacent studio and sat down in front of the kids and sang this off-the-wall spoken word song that I wrote for fun. Engineer Nick Blagona had the wherewithal to set up a two-track recording and it was later sweetened by John Lissauer (best known as the arranger/producer of Leonard Cohen’s “Hallelujah”) and released as a 45 rpm single on Good Noise/Polydor. Polydor’s promo man took me around to radio stations in major markets but the promotion was short-lived when he realized he couldn’t be plugging two “novelty” records at the same time - and the other one was getting a lot more traction - in fact it became a top ten hit. It was called “Spiders and Snakes.” But now the Magic Pick gets another lease on life. And it gave me a reason to get the guitar out of the case in the latter days of my musical life. I hope you enjoy it.

Brian BlainToronto, November 2026
